"Should we use escrow?" is the wrong first question. The right one is: *how much does a buyer stand to lose if the seller doesn't deliver, and how likely is that?* Answer that honestly and the payment model picks itself.

## Three common models

**Direct payment** — money goes straight to the seller. Fast, cheap, simple. Right for low-risk, low-ticket, repeat-relationship sales where fraud is rare and refunds are easy.

**Split / delayed payout** — the platform collects, then pays the seller on a schedule (e.g. weekly). Gives you a window to claw back on chargebacks without full escrow complexity.

**Full escrow** — funds are held and only released on delivery confirmation. Necessary when buyers and sellers are strangers, ticket sizes are meaningful, and "it never arrived" is a realistic dispute. This is what I built for [VaultMart](/#work).

## How to decide

Ask three questions:

1. **What's the average order value?** Higher value tilts toward escrow.
2. **Do buyer and seller have a prior relationship?** Strangers tilt toward escrow.
3. **How bad is a dispute?** Physical goods shipped across the country with no tracking tilt hard toward escrow.

## The cost of escrow

Escrow isn't free. It adds a state machine, a release mechanism, dispute handling, and float management for payouts. If your risk profile doesn't justify it, you're adding complexity for no benefit. I cover the full build in [How to Build an Escrow Marketplace](/blog/how-to-build-an-escrow-marketplace).

## The honest answer

Start with the simplest model your risk allows. You can always add holding logic later — but don't build escrow because it sounds safe if direct payment fits your actual risk.
